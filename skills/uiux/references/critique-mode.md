# Critique mode: one entry, three engines

Read this when asked to review, audit, critique or grade a screen ("what's wrong with this", "is this good", `done`'s Design row, an approval gate before a redesign). A critique judges; it does not edit. Fixing starts only after the reader has the findings and picks what to change.

Every design review runs all three engines, each with its own procedure and judgement, and this skill merges their reports. The engines are this skill's grade and its two modules, `impeccable` and `taste`. A module's procedure is read from its files and followed in full, never through a `Skill` call (neither name is a skill any more), and it is not replaced by this skill's grade.

## Steps

1. **Capture once, for all three.** Screenshots at 375, 768 and 1280px saved as files on disk (a browser tool, a Playwright spec or the project's run skill), plus a short height where a stage is pinned to the screen. A subagent cannot see an image pasted into your conversation. An email or other fixed-width surface is captured at the widths it ships at (375 and its full width).
2. **`impeccable` critique.** Read `../modules/impeccable/MODULE.md` (its Setup, including the `context` launcher) and `../modules/impeccable/reference/critique.md`, and run that critique with its two isolated reviewers in full (design review and detector plus browser evidence), with its Nielsen scoring over the applicable maximum. Its detector alone is not the critique. Its reviewers are isolated and never read `MODULE.md`, so each brief carries the absolute launcher path (`<this skill>/modules/impeccable/scripts/impeccable`) and says that `.agent/skills/impeccable/` in `critique.md` means the module folder. It still saves its report under `.impeccable/critique/` in the project, which is what a later polish and a score trend read; its own Ask-the-User close and trend line fold into the merged close below, and its suggested next step is "ask `uiux` to polish", since `/impeccable` is no longer a command.
3. **`taste` review path, both taste modules.** Read `../modules/taste/MODULE.md` (its "Redesign, restyle or review" step, and `references/critique.md` there) and run its review path: its inspector on the rendered page (`inspect.mjs <url>` measures 1440, 768 and 390px itself), `lint.mjs` on the source (or the rendered HTML when the source is a template `lint.mjs` cannot read), then its own scored critique of the screenshots. Running `inspect.mjs` from a shell or an agent is its meter, not the skill. Fit it to the surface: an app screen takes `--app` so page floors (real pictures, no mono, no thin rules) do not fire on a dashboard; a reading page defends each page-floor exception in one line, as `taste` says; an email, PDF, deck or document gets `lint.mjs` on its rendered HTML and the scored critique, with the inspector "not run: not a web page". A public page also runs `taste-web`'s checker (`../modules/taste-web/MODULE.md`, § 4 step 6, Check and look), and where the two modules' house rules disagree (gradients, effects, dark heroes), the lead module for that page's kind (`../SKILL.md` § Route the job) decides, and the other's finding is reported as a note, not a FAIL.
4. **This skill's grade.** What the other two do not judge: consistency with the app's own language and the sibling screens the reader sees beside this one, mobile-first behaviour, the states beyond the happy path, the five questions in `core-principles.md#tension` (Swap, Squint, Signature, Token, Exclude), and the render evidence at 375, 768 and 1280px against `verification-checklist.md` (overflow, truncation, targets, contrast, states). `taste`'s inspector measures 1440, 768 and 390px, so 375 and 1280 are this grade's to check. For a landing page, portfolio or brochure site, `marketing-pages.md` is the yardstick for structure, proof, type and colour only, skipping its dials and design-read steps. Run it as one `general-purpose` agent without `name:` (a named agent's report never reaches you), report only, carrying the screenshot paths, this skill's reference paths, a one-line audience and job (labelled inferred when it is), and this return shape: one entry per finding with what is seen, the evidence, who is affected and the smallest change, plus a list of anything the captures cannot show.

Steps 2 to 4 can run in the same batch. Merge their reports yourself. If agents are not available, run step 4 inline after the modules and say it was not isolated, since a grade run after the modules' verdicts inherits them.

If the widths cannot be captured, or all you have is a pasted image, run this skill's grade inline, mark each evidence check "not measured: no render at 375/768/1280", and say what would complete it: a URL plus a browser tool, or screenshots saved as files. `impeccable` and `taste` still run on whatever source they can reach, and say what they could not measure.

## What the report holds

- A score line per skill: `impeccable`'s Nielsen score over the applicable maximum, `taste`'s critique score, and this skill's grade. A skill with no score of its own did not run; say "not run" and why, rather than filling the slot with another skill's findings.
- Findings from all three, de-duplicated and ranked by what the reader loses, not by how easy the fix is. Lead with the one that most blocks the screen's job. Each: what is seen, which skill and which heuristic or tell it breaks, who is affected, and the smallest change that would answer it.
- What works, in one or two lines, so a later fix pass does not break it.
- What the captures cannot show (dark mode, real mail clients, motion).
- The decision the user needs to make is the last thing in the reply, asked through `AskUserQuestion` (which findings to fix first), once for all three engines. Claim a trend only from an earlier number on record: `impeccable`'s saved reports under `.impeccable/critique/`, a task-doc line, or the user.
