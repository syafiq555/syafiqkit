# Critique mode: one review

Read this when asked to review, audit, critique or grade a screen ("what's wrong with this", "is this good", `done`'s Design row, an approval gate before a redesign). A critique judges; it does not edit. Fixing starts only after the reader has the findings and picks what to change.

A design review is one review with one report and one score. It carries every check this skill and its modules own (`impeccable`'s heuristics, detector and two isolated reviewers; `taste`'s inspector, lint and critique questions; `taste-web`'s checker; this skill's own checks), run as one procedure rather than as separate reviews merged afterwards. A named critique or audit runs this same review. Module procedures are read from their files, never through a `Skill` call (no module is a skill). In a review this file overrides the module files on scoring, the reviewers' order and the report's shape; a module file still supplies its checks, passes, questions and caps.

## Steps

1. **Capture once.** Screenshots at 375, 768 and 1280px saved as files on disk (a browser tool, a Playwright spec or the project's run skill), plus a short height where a stage is pinned to the screen. A subagent cannot see an image pasted into your conversation. An email or other fixed-width surface is captured at the widths it ships at (375 and its full width); a PDF, deck or document from its rendered pages.
2. **Start reviewer A first**, as `impeccable`'s critique sets it up (`../modules/impeccable/reference/critique.md`): it judges the screenshots against the rubric below, gets no machine output, and finishes before any machine output enters your own context, since that output anchors judgement. Its brief carries the screenshot paths, a one-line audience and job (labelled inferred when it is), and the rubric with its return shape (a 0 to 4 score per heuristic, each with its evidence, then the findings), saying this shape replaces the agent's usual report format. Dispatch A as the project's `product-reviewer` agent when one exists (it loads the project's brief and design rules itself), otherwise `general-purpose`; B is `general-purpose`. Neither gets `name:` (a named agent's report never reaches you).
3. **Run the machine checks that fit the surface** for reviewer B. They are evidence, not the review, and a check that does not apply is listed as "not run: <reason>":
   - `impeccable`'s detector: `<this skill>/modules/impeccable/scripts/impeccable detect --json <target>` (setup in `../modules/impeccable/MODULE.md`; web markup only, so it reads an email's rendered HTML but not a PDF, deck or document).
   - `taste`'s inspector: `node <this skill>/modules/taste/scripts/inspect.mjs <url>` (measures 1440, 768 and 390px itself); `--app` on an app screen so page floors (real pictures, no mono, no thin rules) do not fire on a dashboard; a reading page (docs, guides, articles) defends each page-floor exception in one line, as `taste` says; not run on email, PDF, decks or documents.
   - `taste`'s lint: `lint.mjs` on the source, or on the rendered HTML when the template language is one it cannot read (Blade, Twig, email layouts).
   - On a public page, `taste-web`'s checker (`../modules/taste-web/MODULE.md`, § 4 step 6, Check and look).
4. **Run reviewer B** on the evidence: the step 3 outputs are handed to it in place of running the detector itself (it reruns one only when an output is missing), plus its own browser evidence. Its brief carries those paths, the absolute launcher path, the note that `.agent/skills/impeccable/` in `critique.md` means the module folder, the audience line and the rubric. A and B never see each other's output. Where the detector cannot read the surface (a native app, PDF, deck or document), A runs alone and may be given the lint output, since there is no B to isolate from, under `Method: design review only (detector not applicable, <surface>)`. Without agents, run both inline and open with `impeccable`'s `⚠️ DEGRADED: single-context (<reason>)` banner, as its critique requires.
5. **Merge into one report and one score.** A's heuristic scores are the starting point; B's evidence confirms a score or lowers it, naming the finding that moved it; then the caps below apply. De-duplicate and rank the findings.

## The rubric

One score: `impeccable`'s ten Nielsen heuristics, each 0 to 4, over the applicable maximum (heuristics 7 and 10 may be n/a on a landing page; "not observable" is its own state). The other checks are read into the heuristic they bear on rather than scored apart:
- `taste`'s critique (`../modules/taste/references/critique.md`) and house rules, through aesthetic and minimalist design, consistency and recognition: its 14 passes (§ 1) are how those heuristics are looked at, and reviewers do not produce its own 1 to 10 score (§ 2), since this review has one. Its caps (§ 3) still hold, mapped here: a cap on `taste`'s overall score holds the whole score to that share of the applicable maximum (an open inspector FAIL to 60%, the style tells to 65%); a cap on one of its four criteria holds the heuristics that criterion maps to at 2 of 4 when the cap is 7 or less, and at 3 when it is 7.5 (design to #4 consistency and #8 aesthetic and minimalist design; usability to #1 visibility and #6 recognition; creativity to #8; content to #2 match with the real world). Its fix-first order (§ 5) ranks the findings, and its stop rule (§ 6) bounds any fix rounds that follow: at most three, and a score still under 75% after the third is said plainly.
- This skill's checks: consistency with the app's own language and the sibling screens beside this one; mobile-first behaviour; the states beyond the happy path; the five questions in `core-principles.md#tension` (Swap, Squint, Signature, Token, Exclude); render evidence at 375, 768 and 1280px against `verification-checklist.md` (overflow, truncation, targets, contrast, states).
- For a landing page, portfolio or brochure site, `marketing-pages.md` is the yardstick for structure, proof, type and colour, skipping its dials and design-read steps.
- Where `taste`'s and `taste-web`'s house rules disagree (gradients, effects, dark heroes), the lead module for that page's kind (`../SKILL.md` § Route the job) decides, and the other's finding is a note, not a fault.

If the widths cannot be captured, or all you have is a pasted image, score what can be seen, mark each evidence check "not measured: no render at 375/768/1280", and say what would complete it: a URL plus a browser tool, or screenshots saved as files. The checks that read source (the detector, `lint.mjs`) still run on whatever source they can reach, and say what they could not measure.

## What the report holds

- First line: the method (`impeccable`'s `Method:` header: dual-agent, design review only, or the degraded banner), as its critique requires.
- Then one score line: `N/M (P%)` over the applicable maximum, with its reading (a 4 is genuinely excellent; most real interfaces land at 20 to 32 of 40, 50 to 80%); `taste` passes and caps: applied, caps held "none" or which; and which machine checks ran or "not run: <reason>". "None" is written out, since a blank cannot tell checked-and-clean from never checked.
- Findings de-duplicated and ranked by what the reader loses, not by how easy the fix is. Lead with the one that most blocks the screen's job. Each: what is seen, which heuristic, rule or check it breaks, who is affected, and the smallest change that would answer it.
- What works, in one or two lines, so a later fix pass does not break it.
- What the captures cannot show (dark mode, real mail clients, motion).
- The report is also saved under `.impeccable/critique/` in the project, run as `critique.md` § Persist the Snapshot says (its `.agent/skills/impeccable/` means the module folder, as in step 4); that is what a later polish and a score trend read. Claim a trend only from an earlier number on record there, in a task doc, or from the user.
- The decision the user needs to make is the last thing in the reply, asked once through `AskUserQuestion` (which findings to fix first). The suggested next step is "ask `uiux` to polish", since `/impeccable` is no longer a command.
