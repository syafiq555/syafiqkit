# Worked example: five compliance reports, 23/10/2026

The run this skill was distilled from. Five research reports (Android store rules, iOS store rules, a national privacy law, an analytics vendor, comparable products), 343 KB, were written for a client's clause pack. The reader had asked to "understand everything without missing every single detail".

## What happened, in order

1. **Inventory:** five haiku agents, one per report, produced 1,626 rows. A spot-check of cited lines found them accurate. The agents' own contradiction lists surfaced the biggest structural trap: every report ended with a section 7 "Addendum (client clarifications)" that overrode earlier answers, while each report's bottom-line summary at the top was never updated.
2. **A first page built as a decision board** (14 decision cards, 10 topic panels, a 1,626-row appendix) was hard to read. Two isolated critiques found 95 "Decision needed" flags for 14 real decisions, lines of 121 characters, the answer buried mid-card, sideways scroll on phones, and search bugs. The reader then rejected the decision questions outright: understanding first.
3. **Format chosen by the reader** from four rendered options (slides, talk it through, narrated video, questions and answers): questions and answers. He then asked for the reading technique to be researched; that research (Dunlosky, Rayner, GOV.UK, WCAG, BDA) shaped the recall question, the always-visible answers and the line length.
4. **Seven topic write-ups** by haiku, then **56 claims checked**: 12 confirmed on primary sources, 23 in the project's code or live pages, 13 marked as a report's view, 6 worded differently, 2 not confirmable (an amendment's official PDF and a guideline returned 404).
5. **Four corrections** came from checking against the project's own code and live pages, not from the reports: the published Terms did not promise what the reports said they did (the promise was in an unpublished draft); a "page promises 90 days" finding was wrong because the page and code agreed; a statute section was cited for a consent rule it does not contain (the rule sat in another section); and a vendor sentence ("General text is not masked by default") turned a recommendation into a fact about the live system.
6. **Sample topic rendered and approved** before the rest; final check: 60 screenshots, no overflow, ~71 characters per line, detector warnings 407 → 1.

## What the reader said, and what it taught

| Said | Lesson |
|---|---|
| "we need to understand the documents ... take a step back to really confirm everything" | No decision question until the reader says he understands |
| "im the one who needs to understand not u ?" | Plan around the reader's comprehension; the agent's checking only feeds it |
| "implementation as in the code, in the project, the html doesnt count" | The guide is a reading tool, not product work; build it without waiting |
| "did u use the design skills properly or not ?" | A design read, case-study research and the impeccable critique come before any proposal |
| "their ux also i cant really read also" | Borrowing GOV.UK or store-policy layouts is not enough; ask the reader which format works |
| "im a slow reader too" | Fewer words, one topic per screen, a stop point after each |
| "hv to research the technique" | Name the evidence for each reading choice on the page's design |
