# Casual message incidents

The measured stories behind rules in `SKILL.md`. Each heading names the rule it backs; the rule stays in `SKILL.md` or in `structured-messages.md`, and the story here is its evidence.

## Sender's own messages set the style {#sent-messages-style}

Backs: the sender's own sent messages are the style guide (SKILL.md § Language).

Measured 05/11/2026: a colleague's draft only matched their voice after the session studied WhatsApps they had sent.

## Bubbles and the whitespace they cost {#splitting-bubbles}

Backs: a short reply is one bubble, and a longer one gets a bubble per topic (SKILL.md § What this produces).

Measured 12/11/2026: a six-point WhatsApp drafted as five bubbles came back "too many spaces between the texts", and two bubbles were accepted.

Measured 21/11/2026: a two-sentence Chat thread reply in two bubbles was refused at send, "for this simple message no need the extra whitespace, combine them".

## Closing line on a question list {#closing-line}

Backs: no closing line the sender doesn't use, including a line telling the recipient how to answer (SKILL.md § What this produces).

Measured 2026-10-26: a three-question WhatsApp to a client ended "Reply ikut nombor pun ok 👍", and the sender's own version simply stopped after question 3.

## Alternatives as bullets {#alternatives-bullets}

Backs: three or more alternatives keep `- ` bullets (SKILL.md § What this produces).

Measured 23/11/2026 (issue #36): four guesses at which part of a colleague's use case was missing came back joined by commas and "atau" in one sentence, and the sender asked to keep the bullets.

## Opener about the message {#opener}

Backs: the first line is part of the message, not about it (SKILL.md § What this produces).

Measured 18/11/2026: a scope posted for a colleague to paste into a quotation came back "no need to say can paste as is".

Measured 20/11/2026: across several replies in one thread, the openers narrating the sender's checks made recipients call them automated ("nampak cliche", "balas semua auto"), even when they had asked for the check.

## Names the session coined {#coined-names}

Backs: names the session coined don't travel; a past meeting is named by what happened in it, and a tracker row by what it asks (SKILL.md § What this produces).

Measured 23/11/2026: "21 oct" came back "reword it in a way he can flash back", and "row 2, 3, 11 and 12" came back "still hv a lot of numbers".

## Shortest answer to a draft question {#shortest-answer}

Backs: a colleague's question about their own draft gets the shortest answer that settles it (references/structured-messages.md#reply-to-their-message).

Measured 18/11/2026: "which point is this assumption for?" got a four-part mapping; the colleague replied "er" with a screenshot already laid out right, and the next draft came back "keep it much more simpler".

## Promise work the session can do now {#done-before-promise}

Backs: work this session can do now is done before the draft, not promised in it (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 2026-10-14: a draft promised a document revision "in the next version", and the sender asked why it was not simply updated before sending.

## Question the sender can answer {#sender-answers-first}

Backs: a question the sender can answer never goes to the recipient (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 25/11/2026: a message to the sender's mother asked whether her old shop listing and her books were hers, and came back "u think i don't know".

## Checking a present-tense production claim {#live-system}

Backs: "it does X now" is checked against the live system, not the build (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 2026-10-22: a team note said typed old codes "credit" a partner, while that partner's record did not exist on either server yet; the sender caught it only by asking "show evidence of ur claims?".

## Figures and events from the record {#record-not-reading}

Backs: a fact about what a user did, or a figure quoted, comes from the record (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 22/11/2026: a team reply said a lister "never clicked next", taken from a colleague's account, and had to be corrected after the events showed one click. Its "5 of the last 8 drafts" came back as "a bit bias, check againts all", and the 90-day count was 14 of 55.

Measured 25/11/2026: an agency named after a person was called "the landlord" in a client reply, and the user asked "what landlord one? are u sure?".

## A link the recipient cannot open {#link-check}

Backs: a link in the draft is checked the way the recipient will open it (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 09/11/2026: a team message pointed at a slide deck only its owner could open, and the sender asked "i dont think the slide able to view ?".

## Third party's words quoted {#third-party-words}

Backs: what a third party said travels as their words (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 2026-10-22: a note relaying a client's two answers came back as "give evidence from their replies".

Measured 23/11/2026: a reply arguing the recipient had decided something came back "Quote what he said".

## Thread reply length on Chat {#chat-length}

Backs: a Chat send holds about 1,000 characters, so a longer draft is shown already split (references/structured-messages.md#chat-send-markdown).

Measured 23/11/2026: an approved three-paragraph thread reply was refused at preview for length and posted as two.

## Bullets on WhatsApp {#whatsapp-bullets}

Backs: on WhatsApp bullets start with `- `, not `* ` (SKILL.md § Syntax).

Measured 03/11/2026: a client-group WhatsApp drafted with `* ` bullets came back "change to whatsapp format", and the accepted copy changed only the bullets.

## Code formatting for typed strings {#code-formatting}

Backs: anything the recipient must copy or type goes in code formatting (SKILL.md § Syntax).

Measured 17/11/2026: a WhatsApp asking a client to add a webhook (an endpoint and four event names) came back "make it easier for code one may use ticks or triple ticks".

## Client reply explains internals {#client-reply-internals}

Backs: a reply to a client reports the result, not how the system works, and the team's build state stays out of it (SKILL.md § Replying to their message).

Measured 24/11/2026: an account-closure "done" email lost its cancelled-bills line, "no need to explain the bill", then "Bucky can let them know", "no need mention bucky".

Measured 23/11/2026: a "done, invoices linked" reply grew a paragraph on why linked invoices stay off the statement of account and an RM 1,000 to chase; the sender answered "this seems like can confuse them" and "just fix for the apac, no need explain to them, they dont care, they only want result".

Measured 27/11/2026: "dont mention switch lol, they dont [know] we pre build it already".

## Suggestion wording varies {#suggestion-wording}

Backs: a question to a client carries a varied suggestion (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 27/11/2026: nine "Which one is it?" questions were reworded to suggestions, then "Will it work for you if" on every line came back "a bit excessive".

## Client ask needs a question {#statement-ask}

Backs: a client ask written as a statement is still an ask (SKILL.md § Speaking for the sender).

Measured 27/11/2026: a reply ending "once your team fixes them on their side, we'll update" needed rewording to "Will your team be able to …?".

## Unfenced draft {#fence-unfenced}

Backs: the draft goes in one fence so the sender gets a copy button (SKILL.md, the fence paragraph near the end).

Measured 16/11/2026: an unfenced WhatsApp draft came back "cant it be something like i can click copy ... like gchat format way?", the same reversal `gchat-format` records for its own no-fence default.

## Draft that ends the turn {#ends-the-turn}

Backs: the draft ends the turn (SKILL.md, the last paragraph).

Measured 2026-10-08: a WhatsApp reply was drafted and `/done` was invoked in the same reply, and the sender had to ask "where the msg?".
